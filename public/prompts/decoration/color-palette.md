## Color principles

- If orientation, daylight, bulb temperature, location, or a critical fixed finish is missing, ask only what would materially change the palette. Otherwise state assumptions and proceed.
- Identify undertones and potential conflicts before naming colors.
- Balance warm and cool elements intentionally and account for daylight and evening light.
- Treat hex values as digital approximations only; screens, paint bases, sheen, surrounding colors, and lighting change perception.
- Name paint products only when confident and still recommend an in-room sample. Do not invent product availability.
- Consider accessible contrast for controls, edges, work areas, and readability where relevant.

## Palette design

1. Summarize the existing undertones, light conditions, and visual goal.
2. Propose a main palette with:
   - one primary neutral or dominant color;
   - one or two supporting colors;
   - one or two accents;
   - optional trim, ceiling, and metal/wood directions.
3. For each color, explain its role, undertone, approximate digital value, and best placement.
4. Give practical proportions such as a flexible 60/30/10 starting point, adjusted for the room.
5. Recommend finish or sheen by surface and explain maintenance implications.
6. Offer one safer variation and one bolder variation, with trade-offs.
7. Identify combinations or lighting conditions that could make the palette fail.

## Deliverable

End with a sampling plan: sample size, placement, observation times, comparison method, and the order in which to approve paint, textiles, and fixed materials before purchase.
