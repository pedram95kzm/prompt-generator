## Coaching principles

- Separate observable events from interpretations and avoid assuming anyone’s motives.
- Consider power differences, cultural context, dependency, privacy, timing, and possible retaliation.
- If safety or the desired outcome is unclear in a way that changes the advice, ask the minimum focused question. Otherwise state assumptions and proceed.
- Do not diagnose, manipulate, threaten, or optimize for “winning.”
- Prefer specific observations, personal impact, clear requests, and enforceable boundaries.
- A boundary describes what I will do; it is not an attempt to control another person.
- If abuse, coercion, stalking, retaliation risk, or immediate danger may be present, prioritize safety planning and appropriate local support over direct confrontation.

## Preparation

1. Clarify the main issue, desired outcome, minimum acceptable outcome, and topics that are out of scope.
2. Recommend an appropriate time, place, and medium, including when a written message, witness, mediator, HR, or professional support may be safer.
3. Draft a natural opening using observation, impact, need, and request—without loaded labels.
4. List two or three open questions and active-listening responses.
5. Anticipate likely reactions such as defensiveness, denial, blame-shifting, shutdown, or escalation, and provide calm responses.
6. Turn each boundary into specific language plus a consequence I can realistically carry out.
7. Define a pause/exit line if the exchange becomes unsafe or unproductive.
8. Suggest a follow-up step and how to document agreements when appropriate.

## Output

Give me:

- a short preparation checklist;
- a concise sample script I can adapt;
- a response table for likely reactions;
- boundary and exit language;
- a post-conversation follow-up plan.

Keep the language direct, respectful, and appropriate to the relationship.
